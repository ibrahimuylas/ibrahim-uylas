Call it trekking or call it hiking: you need socks. Going for a walk without them is a bit like going without your underpants, if you ask me. But why hiking socks in particular? Is the point of paying a small fortune for special socks instead of wearing your everyday ones just to show off?

\- I can answer that: no, it isn’t.

\- I’m asking again, then, what if not?

When it comes to nature walks, one of the three most important things is socks. The other two are health and a good pair of boots. The cotton socks we use in our daily lives are not suitable for trekking and hiking. The main reason for this is that they don't absorb sweat. If your feet get wet with sweat, you’ll feel cold, even if the weather is warm enough not to. Therefore, they don’t provide the necessary comfort and, due to their thin structure, they are prone to collecting moisture, leading to your feet becoming wet. The better your trekking/hiking shoes and socks, the more comfortable and enjoyable your walk will be.

### Benefits of Trekking Socks

\- They’re soft and comfortable.

\- It provides excellent insulation and protects your feet from getting wet.

\- It helps to release the sweat on your foot and provides good ventilation. It also keeps your foot warm.

\- Merino wool socks, on the other hand, provide significantly better insulation and adapt very well to your body temperature. They don’t lose their insulating properties even when wet, keeping your feet warm in the cold and providing an exceptionally comfortable feeling. These types of socks are rarely made entirely from wool; they usually contain around 35-40% merino wool. Furthermore, these socks are resistant to odor.

\- Some socks have more support in the heel and toe areas. These socks, which are separated into left and right, fit better on the foot. I'm just making a general observation. For example, the Sealskinz Hiking socks in the photo don't have this feature, but they fit the foot very well. Nevertheless, I always wear the same sock on the same foot to get my foot shape used to it. How do I tell the difference? I distinguish them by noting that one has a label and the other doesn't :)

### Trekking socks available in Turkey

I’m very sorry to say that I couldn’t find walking socks in Turkey, at stores selling camping supplies, of the quality available in London, or even close to it. Yes, I checked about four or five different sites. Many had nice socks, but they were out of stock. It’s not hard to understand why these socks aren’t sold: when it comes to hiking, people don’t want to spend much on socks. They just brush them off. No demand, so not many products available. My advice: when buying socks, check what material they’re made from. Avoid cotton socks. Yes, cotton is more comfortable, but it absorbs sweat and, over time, leads to moisture buildup and friction, making your feet wet. Socks of a higher quality than cotton are usually made from acrylic and polyester, but these don’t hold up well. They don’t wick moisture, but they stink like crazy. You won’t believe how badly your feet can smell after a day’s walk. And after a while, your shoes might start to smell too. So if you find socks made from merino wool, grab them.
