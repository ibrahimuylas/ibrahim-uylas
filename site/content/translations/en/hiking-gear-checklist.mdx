The equipment list for a hiking trip varies depending on the route’s duration, weather conditions, and difficulty. The aim is not to pack everything imaginable, but rather to ensure that the bag contains the necessary items, while maintaining balance between adapting to weather changes and ensuring a comfortable hike.

## Quick checklist

- Walking shoes and spare socks
- Water, snacks, and the option to purify water when needed.
- Rain/wind layer and season-appropriate spare clothing
- Offline map, a charged phone, and a portable charger.
- The first aid kit, personal medications, a whistle, and a headlamp.
- Sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses.
- Garbage bag and a small repair kit.
- Depending on the route: trekking poles, gaiters and an emergency blanket

If you’re planning a day trip, you can start with [What Is Hiking? A Guide to Day Hikes](/hiking-ne-demek/), and if you’re planning a multi-day trip, you can start with [What Is Trekking? A Beginner’s Guide](/trekking-ne-demek/).

## Hiking shoes

The shoes should be suitable for your foot, the ground, and the weight you will carry. Do not use new shoes on a long trip; test them with short walks and check the sole support. You can refer to the “[How to Choose Trekking Shoes](/trekking-ayakkabisi-nasil-olmali/)” guide for more details.

## Sock and foot care

Cotton socks can trap sweat and leave your feet damp for long periods. Wicking walking socks and a dry spare pair in your bag will boost comfort. If you notice a hot spot or friction during a break, take action before blisters form.

## Tozluk

Gaiters help keep small stones, mud, snow and wet plants out of your boots or trouser cuffs. You won’t need them on every trail, but they’re more useful on loose ground, in snow or in dense brush. See my [waterproof hiking gaiters](/su-gecirmez-yuruyus-tozlugu/) article for an example.

## Layered clothing

A moisture-wicking inner layer, a warming mid-layer, and rain/wind protection help adapt to changing weather conditions. Cotton clothing dries quickly when wet. For choosing what to wear, refer to the guide: [how to dress for nature walks](/doga-yuruyuslerinde-nasil-giyinilmelidir/).

## Hat, buff, and glasses

The hat and sunglasses protect your eyes and face in sunny weather. A buff is a lightweight item that can be used in various ways in sunny, windy, and cool weather. You can find information on how to use a buff at [What Is a Buff and How Do You Use It?](/buff-nedir-ne-ise-yarar/).

## Backpack

For a day hike, most people need 15–30 liters, while for multi-day trekking, the amount needed depends on the type of accommodation, ranging from 35–70 liters. Volume alone isn’t the sole deciding factor; it’s more important that the bag fits your torso, that the load is evenly distributed, and that the total weight is suitable for the route.

Keep the frequently used layers of water and rain in easily accessible compartments. Carry heavy items close to your back, and avoid leaving unnecessary items dangling outside the bag.

## Water and food

Plan your water needs according to the weather, distance, altitude, and reliable sources along the route. Do not trust any fountain or stream shown on the map on its own; it may have dried up seasonally. For long routes, keep a backup plan for a filter or other suitable purification method.

Choose foods that are easy to consume and provide energy during your walk, foods you’ve tried before. Allow a little extra food for emergencies beyond the planned duration.

## Navigation and communication

Download an offline map to your phone and try to open the route file before you start moving. Carry a portable charger, and take into account that the battery may drain faster in cold weather. Share your route and estimated arrival time with someone you trust. Remember that this plan will not serve as a substitute for calling for help in areas where the phone has no signal.

## First aid and emergency supplies

A small first aid kit, personal medications, a whistle, a headlamp, and an emergency blanket take up little space in the bag. But knowing how to use the equipment is just as important as carrying it. First aid training is more valuable than simply having the equipment list.

## Using trekking poles

The walking stick can be helpful for balance, especially during landings, on loose ground, and when walking with a heavy bag. Adjust its height according to the terrain, and avoid keeping your hands constantly locked in the strap. You can find more details at [what trekking poles are used for](/yuruyus-batonu-ne-ise-yarar/).

## Additions for multi-day hikes

In the trekking plan, this list is supplemented with accommodation and meal equipment:

- Book a tent or accommodation reservation based on the conditions.
- A sleeping mat and a sleeping bag suited to the overnight temperature
- If you’re taking a stove, a safe setup for fuel and cooking
- Spare clothing, a more detailed food and water plan.
- Stages’ supply and exit points

For help choosing a sleep system, see [what to look for in a sleeping bag](/uyku-tulumu-alirken-nelere-dikkat-edilmelidir/).

## Final check

Review your gear list against the forecast, trail difficulty and the people you’ll be walking with. Route information, permissions and local restrictions can change. Before leaving, go through my [pre-hike checks](/doga-yuruyuslerinde-dikkat-edilmesi-gerekenler/).
