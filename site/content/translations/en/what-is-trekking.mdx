Trekking is a long walk in nature, usually divided into stages over several days, with overnight stops to plan. The exact definition varies by country and route. What sets trekking apart from a day hike is that the journey continues the next day and needs more preparation.

## At a glance

- **Duration:** Usually more than one day
- **Plan:** Start and finish, stages, water and overnight stops
- **Pack:** Roughly 35–70 litres, depending on where you sleep
- **Pace:** Adjusted to the weight you carry, elevation and terrain
- **Main difference:** Hiking is usually a day trip with lighter equipment

## What does trekking involve?

Trekking involves more than walking a long distance. You divide the route into days, keep an eye on weather and trail conditions, plan where to get water and carry camping gear if you need it. You can sleep in a tent or stay at guesthouses or mountain huts.

A tent, sleeping mat, sleeping bag, spare clothes, food and water quickly add weight to your pack. Plan each day around elevation, terrain, weather, pack weight and hours of daylight, as well as distance.

## What is the difference between trekking and hiking?

Hiking usually means a walk in nature that you complete in a day with a smaller pack. A trek follows a route over several days and involves planning where to stay overnight. The terms are used differently in different sources. The preparation the route requires matters more than what you call the walk.

For a closer look, read [the differences between trekking and hiking](/trekking-ile-hiking-arasindaki-farklar-nelerdir/).

## What equipment do you need for trekking?

What you need depends on the season and where you plan to sleep. Start with these essentials:

- Well-fitting hiking footwear and spare socks
- A rain and wind layer
- Your route, an offline map and a backup power source
- Enough water, a way to treat it and a meal plan
- A first-aid kit, head torch and whistle
- A tent, mat and sleeping bag suitable for the conditions if you are camping

See the [hiking gear checklist](/doga-yuruyusleri-icin-gerekli-ekipmanlar/) for a full list and [how to choose trekking shoes](/trekking-ayakkabisi-nasil-olmali/) for footwear.

## How do you plan your first trek?

1. Choose a well-marked route that you can leave early if you need to.
2. Set daily distances based on elevation and the pack you will carry, rather than your usual walking pace.
3. Check water sources using more than one source and keep some spare capacity to carry water.
4. Check the forecast, sunset time and local access restrictions again before leaving.
5. Share your route and return plan with someone you trust.
6. If conditions change, turning back is a valid decision.
