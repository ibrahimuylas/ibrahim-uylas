---
title: "The Benefits of Hiking"
description: "Good for the heart, the legs, the soul… Listen to me, I sound like a salesman on the Eminönü ferry. So what do we actually get out of walking?"
---
To my brothers and sisters, take a look at this amazing thing in my hand – trekking/hiking, or simply nature walks – which is incredibly beneficial for the heart, kidneys, liver, spleen, mind, and overall health. I actually felt like I was selling these nature walks on the Eminönü ferry. I remember reading an article a long time ago titled “The Benefits of Trekking,” which contained similar descriptions. It’s truly amazing how beneficial regular nature walks are. Doctors and experts are saying this. Let me list the positive aspects I’ve experienced as someone who regularly engages in nature walks.

First of all, I want to emphasize that it’s incredibly beneficial for overall health. Let me give you a few examples from my life.

* I can climb the stairs of my house without getting out of breath. There are quite a few steps, actually.
* When we play five-a-side, I’m still running like a dog in the 59th minute. So yes, it does wonders for my stamina.
* During the summer and winter months, when microbes are everywhere and people are getting sick – and I would often be very ill myself – I manage to get through it in a much lighter way.
* I wake up feeling much more refreshed in the mornings.
* It aids digestion. You’ll likely notice that your digestive system is working much harder when you’re walking.

Regarding my mental health, I’ve observed positive effects. As I’ve been doing trekking/hiking, I’ve become wonderfully eccentric :)

* I feel more cheerful and more confident.
* If I spend my weekends camping or going on a nature walk, I arrive at work on Monday feeling more motivated, and even with a busy workload afterward, my motivation remains better.
* A week of walking keeps me going for a fortnight, I reckon.
* [Trekking](/trekking-ne-demek/) and [hiking](/hiking-ne-demek/) are among the most popular outdoor activities because they don’t require exceptional fitness or detailed technical knowledge, the risks of injury and similar problems are low, and they don’t cost much.

It can help reduce the risk of weight gain.

* I’ve got a serious sweet tooth, so I can put on weight pretty easily. All the effort that goes into a good hike helps tip the balance back.
