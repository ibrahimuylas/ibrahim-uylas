I don’t know why I haven’t written about Yedigöller, even though I’ve been there three times in the last two years. At one point, I did intend to write about it. The best time to visit is autumn. You can see every shade of orange. As the name suggests, there are seven lakes. Those who visit in autumn struggle to find the seventh. “We’ve counted six. Where’s the seventh?” you might ask. That small lake tucked away by the stream dries up in autumn due to low water levels—that’s why it’s called Kurugöl. Besides the seven lakes, there’s the Dilek fountain, a waterfall, and the Gülen rocks. You can walk around the lakes and through the forest. In designated camping areas, you’re allowed to pitch a tent (for a fee). On weekends, it gets very crowded due to the influx of day-trippers, so I’d recommend visiting on a weekday if you can.

If you would like to read my trip account from two years ago, please come; [My Seven Lakes trekking adventure](/yedigoller-trekking-tek-basima-ilk-yolculuk/)

### Bolu Yedigöller: Where and how to get there?

While traveling from İstanbul to Bolu, you will see the Yedigöller sign at the western toll road junction of Bolu. After this, there will be no further signs. So, how should you proceed? The best option is to follow the route on the Google Maps below. Those coming from Ankara can reach here via Mengen. The road between Mengen and Yedigöller is partially unpaved, while the road from Bolu to Yedigöller is entirely asphalt. They have paved the 40 km unpaved road. Although there are some curves, it’s a great opportunity to see the forests near Bolu.

### Bolu Yedigöller camping area information

##### Available facilities

- The national parks are under protection. Therefore, the camping area has a fee. The fee for a tent is 20TL.
- The entry fee by car is 12 TL.
- Prayer room
- Toilets
- Parking
- Picnic table
- Bungalow Accommodation

##### Facilities that are unavailable

- Grocery shop
- Restaurant
- Café

Note: This information dates back to October 2015.

- Starting at Büyük Lake, the route goes around Dipsiz Lake, past the Dilek fountain and waterfall, then past the Gülen rocks and on to the other lakes. It returns to Büyük Lake by the road.
- From Dipsiz Lake it’s uphill all the way to Sazlı Lake. From there, follow the road downhill to Büyük Lake.
- The forest and lake offer a beautiful and easy trail.
- Campgrounds and around the lakes, there are fountains available. You can meet your water needs here.
