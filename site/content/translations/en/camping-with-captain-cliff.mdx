Cliff Jacobson, who lives in North America, is considered one of the most respected outdoor sports writers. He is a professional kayaker and has extensive experience in camping. He has undertaken extended canoeing and camping trips, becoming a part of the canoeing and camping lifestyle. This book on camping provides a wealth of important information, including how to use your equipment effectively, how to build a fire, how to use cutting tools, how to choose a suitable stove, how to tie practical and easy knots, and, most importantly, how to protect yourself from potential dangers.

If you have an interest in camping, or if you've been doing this for years, I still recommend this book to you. You won't find anything else like it. If you, like me – who has been camping for years (though it’s not quite 30 yet :) – are reading it, you'll find many things familiar. When reading it, you'll be forming sentences like "yes, I know, I'm doing this, it's become a part of my routine," and you'll be able to say, "wow, there's even this!" and "I learned something new, which was great!" and you'll encounter information that will surprise you. Many of us haven't experienced a 30-day camping life, or a 45-day canoeing trip. That's why Cliff's experiences are so important for us. Personally, I planned a 21-day solo journey in Lycia, but I could only manage about 11 days. I gave in to loneliness and hadn't prepared a good nutrition plan for more than that. Cliff shares many tips to make your journey more comfortable, such as wearing two pairs of socks in your boots to prevent blisters. Cliff once came across a brown bear on the tundra in Canada and the approach he took worked. The bear went its way, and Cliff went his. I was particularly surprised to learn that bears can't see very well.

If you’re a beginner, you can be sure that you will learn a great deal. If you’re doing this as a novice, you’ll not only refresh your knowledge but also learn many new and useful things. While reading this book won’t turn you into a super camper, it will certainly be a good starting point. I believe that learning from the experiences of someone who has spent months camping and canoeing is a great opportunity.
