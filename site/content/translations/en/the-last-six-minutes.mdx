---
title: "The Last Six Minutes"
description: "One evening in London I’m cycling home through the crowd. Something happens on the way that makes me think about what I’d say if I had six minutes left."
---
One evening in London I’m on my bike, picking my way slowly through the crowd on my way home. A man comes up fast beside me. I think he’s about to steal the bike. Then he pulls a knife, stabs me in the middle of my back and runs. I’m still trying to take it in as I fall onto my back. People crowd round in a panic. “Please, call the police and an ambulance.” How deep did the knife go? How much blood have I lost? I can’t move or feel a thing. I ask the first man who reaches me how my back looks. He says the wound’s deep and I’m losing a lot of blood. I lean back against my rucksack, trying to press on the wound. Someone says, “He’s got six minutes at most.” Six minutes. I know nobody’s going to reach me in that time. What do I do? I take the man’s right hand in mine and pull him close. “These are probably my last moments. I’m not sad about dying young. I feel I’ve lived enough. I’m not panicking either. I always knew this day would come. I want you to thank everyone who’s come into my life, everyone I love, my family. We were all going our own ways and somehow our paths crossed. We made good memories. Tell them how glad I am they were part of my story in this short life. Tell them I love them. If I could have lived a little…” Everything goes dark. The voices fade. So this is it, I think. I’m on my way. I slowly let go of his hand. And just as I’m slipping peacefully into eternity, I open my eyes. I’ve fallen asleep reading my book.

It was a dream. But if I really had six minutes left in this world and something to say, I think it would come out much like that. I wanted to write it down before I’d even washed my face :)

Here’s to many more days when we know what this life is worth.
