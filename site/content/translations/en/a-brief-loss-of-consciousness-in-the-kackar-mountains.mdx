August 2013

After a night’s sleep of one hour, we began a car journey from Erzurum at 6 AM and arrived at the Olgunlar valley in the Kaçkar mountains at 12 PM. The weather is pleasant, with light clouds. We’re in high spirits and eager to start trekking as soon as possible. We left the car in the valley and set off on our way, with the intention of crossing Naletleme pass and reaching the other side of the mountain as quickly as possible. We have three days available. Our plan is to cross the pass on the first day and set up camp, then explore the lakes on the other side of the mountain, Yukari Kavrun and Ayder valley on the second day, and finally, cross the pass again on the third day to return.

The path leading to Geçide follows a trail through a valley. Thanks to the stream running alongside us, we're drinking cool water and don't need to carry it. After walking a bit within the valley, we can see the summit of the Kaçkar mountains and Naletleme pass. The snow and clouds on the mountain's peak are exciting. When we arrive at the entrance of the pass, we've walked for approximately 4 hours. The path is rocky and covered in clouds at the entrance to the pass, making it difficult to see how the path continues. We're exhausted as a team – Recep, Enes, and I. We had about 1 hour of sleep, a 6-hour car journey, and a roughly 4-hour trail climb with backpacks weighing around 15–16 kg. It would be nice to stop here, but our plans are being disrupted. We ventured into the pass with the idea that if we walked for another 3 hours, we could cross the pass and camp by the lake at around 7 that evening. The visibility is about 10 metres, and because of the fog, we can only see the rocks and stones. There's no trail; just a row of stacked stones (babalar). We continue to follow them and ascend. The altitude is increasing, and the temperature is getting colder. On 15 August, we're getting cold because there's snow with us. We're continuing on the path by adding another layer of clothing. There's no sign of life around – no bird sounds, no wind, no movement.

We’re at approximately 3200 meters. Cirit? We can’t even lift our arms from exhaustion. We’re looking for a relatively flat and earthy spot to set up a tent, but unfortunately, there isn’t one. We realized where this pass gets its name – it’s 8 o’clock, and the weather is starting to darken. We’re taking out our flashlights and continuing on our way. Everything’s fine, but there’s also fog. If there wasn’t fog, at least we’d know where we are. I’m checking the GPS, but it’s not making any sense. We’re somewhere near a lake, but we don’t know how to get there. Because everywhere is rocky and uneven. And on top of that, none of us have trekking boots. We’ve come wearing our regular athletic shoes. We’re doing a workout at the gym, right? Why would we need to buy and wear trekking boots? We can feel every single stone on the ground – right down to the tiniest curves :)

The birds don’t fly, people can’t pass, and life can’t be born here – we’re trying to get out, but we can’t. I’m really hoping a wolf comes out of this thick fog. At that moment, Enes experienced a sudden loss of consciousness and I thought of him as my friend. I wondered, ‘What is he doing here? Where does he belong?’ But there’s no stopping – we keep going. The man is so focused on the path that he’s just walking. Then I stopped in front of Recep. As we were moving along, Recep wondered, ‘Who’s leading the way? Is there someone else?’ He shouted, ‘Who’s leading?’ We had no idea he was talking to himself. Two minutes later, I lost my father. Oh my god. We’re going crazy. The GPS isn’t helping at all. We’re in a really strange place. We’re close to Göle, but how are we going to get there? If we fall on the rocks in this darkness, we’re completely finished. ‘Oh God.’ I think we’re coming to you. Morale is zero. If we could find a slightly flat place, we’d lie down and sleep. But there’s nothing. This cursed place has no ground. If there was, we’d already be collapsing. We stopped for a moment and looked around. We’re practically dying from exhaustion, but it’s difficult to spend the night here. We’ll either set up a tent here, or, after a 2-hour break, we’ll continue on our way. While I was looking for a place to set up a tent, searching for a place where a tent could really fit, I saw a dense thicket – about 30-40cm high. We’re putting the tent on that thicket, and then, after having something to eat and drink, I’m noting down these words: ‘Let’s not think about what we’re doing right now. Let’s just sleep. Will we continue, or will we turn back when we wake up. Let’s just sleep.’

In this journey, which puts our lives at risk, we awoke to a sunny morning after 13 hours of uninterrupted sleep, and the trip continued in a pleasant manner. We rested and the sunshine was very welcome.

When we consider the lessons we’ve learned from this event, there’s something we need to keep in mind: you shouldn’t set off when you’re not well-rested, you shouldn’t force yourself if the plan isn’t working, you won’t hike without your trekking boots, you’ll stop when you’re tired, you’ll be a man, a real man :)
