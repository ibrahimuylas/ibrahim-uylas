## Karadere–Kınık: A flatter day in the Patara plain

This guide describes the 15 km fourth stage, running from Karadere to Pydnee and on to Kınık. Following yesterday’s steep descent into Gavurağalı, this section is characterized by a predominantly road and plain terrain, and serves as a passage day where the pace was reduced in 2014.

> Historical field record: The following practical information is based on the experience of 23 September 2014. Water, facility, transportation, and camping conditions should not be considered current information.

### Stage summary

- **Stage:** 4 of 11
- **Start and finish:** Karadere – Kınık
- **Waypoints:** Pydnee and the start of Patara Beach
- **Distance:** 15 km based on signposted distances
- **Walking time:** The total walking time was not recorded.
- **Walked:** 23 September 2014
- **Current conditions:** No recent verification was available for this guide.

### Terrain, difficulty and navigation

In 2014, the text describes a relatively easy walk from Karadere to Kınık center, primarily on paved roads and flat ground. It notes that the pace was particularly slow due to aching feet and back pain, and that there was no difficulty in finding a direction.

### Water and food — 2014 record

This section of the guide indicates that there have been no issues with water and logistics support. Due to the absence of a recorded, specific water source or business name, a current supply point cannot be determined.

### Accommodation, camping and ways off the route — 2014 record

It was noted that suitable camping locations were not found in Kınık, as the area was covered with greenhouses. The author traveled from Kınık by minibus to Patara, spending the night at Medusa Camping – this represents the historical departure record, and is not current route or service information.

### Notable places

Pydnee is the starting and registered point of the Patara beach and Kınık plain route. Previously, Letoon and Xanthos were referred to as ancient cities surrounding the hiking trail.

### What I learned on this stage

The fourth day’s log shows that it involved a more manageable day, with a reduced pace, and the ability to use transport at the end of the stage to rest, as part of the walking plan following a difficult day.
