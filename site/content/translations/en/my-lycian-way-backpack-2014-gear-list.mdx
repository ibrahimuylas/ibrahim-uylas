This list records the tent, mat, clothing, first aid, and other backpack equipment I chose to use when hiking the Lycian Way on September 20, 2014, as part of my efforts to reduce my weight.

## 2014 backpack list

Okay, how did I prepare my bag when I embarked on my Lycian journey on September 20, 2014? 

The Lycian Way is a long trail. At the time I knew it as the longest in Türkiye, and I’d planned on 21 days. On a walk like that you count the grams. Even an extra 10 g starts to feel heavier as the days go by, so I thought hard about everything I packed.

* First I replaced my tent. I’d already been planning to buy a good one. I looked for a lightweight tent I’d be comfortable with on the Lycian Way and settled on one of the lightest tents in the world, the Ferrino Light-Tent 2. [Read more about the tent here](/ferrino-lightent-2-kamp-cadiri/).
* I wanted a decent night’s sleep. Those green army mats give you insulation but you can still wake up aching all over. I’d be in the tent most nights, so I decided to get an inflatable mat. I had my eye on the Thermarest X-Term L, the daddy of them all. Then I saw the price. What I actually bought was the more affordable Ferrino X-Tech, a 3.8 cm inflatable mat with the insulation and cushioning I wanted. [Here’s my video review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xyDx1ZmiHdM).
* During the time I was traveling, the chance of rain was low. However, I still decided not to take any risks and brought along lightweight, space-saving disposable raincoats.
* I packed just 1 change of clothes. I thought I could alternate outfits, wash them when I found water and buy more when I reached civilisation. That’s why I figured 1 spare outfit was enough.
* Long walks can strain your muscles, so I took a knee support.
* Pen and a notebook to write down my daily memories.
* I bought a camera, a monopod, and a small tripod.
* A torch that runs on AA batteries. I might need a light after dark, and I figured I’d be able to find replacement batteries along the way.
* I was using a tablet as my phone. The large screen was an advantage. Although it was a bit heavy, I preferred to carry it with me.
* I packed a small knife in case I needed it, and my pipe. I like my little pleasures. That pipe will be my walking companion.

Here’s what everything weighed.

* Bag: 2100 g
* Tent: 1700 g
* Inflatable Mat: 1450 g
* Sleeping bag: 1400 g
* Tablet : 580 g
* Clothing: 300 g
* Pen + Notebook : 250 g
* Knee support + waterproof: 250 g
* Biscuit: 200 g
* Monopod + Tripod: 180 g
* Knife + pipe: 150 g
* Torch: 150 g
* Other miscellaneous: 300 g
* Water: 2000 g

That came to about 11 kg on paper. Just about ideal.

 

One thing I still can’t explain. By the time I set off, the pack weighed 11 kg before I put any water in it. I needed to carry at least 3 litres, and on the trail I averaged 13–14 kg.
