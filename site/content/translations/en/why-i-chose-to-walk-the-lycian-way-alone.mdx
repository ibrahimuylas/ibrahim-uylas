This personal writing explains why I wanted to undertake the 2014 Lycian Way route alone – through my thoughts about freedom, taking responsibility, listening to nature, and living my own story.

## In 2014, I decided to walk alone.

9 September 2014

In recent days, the question I've most naturally encountered is "Why are you going to Lycia alone?" Although my first thought was "I couldn't find anyone to walk with me at the same pace for such a long distance in 21 days," I realised this was merely an excuse. For I hadn't set out in search of a travel companion. When I pushed myself a little further, I came to realise that I actually wanted to go alone. So why did I want to go alone? Why would someone who enjoys sharing many things in life choose to go alone? Why did I only enjoy trekking alone? I knew that setting out alone was very risky, yet why did I still go? As I tried to find an answer, the 'me' within me began to ask me even more questions. Although I thought I might drown in these questions, I realised that they were actually guiding me towards the answer.

Initially, I really enjoyed going on treks alone. Because, I was taking the risk myself and not relying on anyone else. This gave me a greater sense of freedom and control. As a result, I was creating my own experiences. When I was alone, I listened to, observed, and experienced nature more deeply. I enjoyed spending time alone, having long conversations with myself. I made new friends on the trails and lived through exciting adventures. I loved setting up my own tent alone at the foot of a mountain or within a forest, building a fire, and gazing at the sky, lost in deep thought. I went on these trips alone, but I carried everyone and everything with me, in my heart and mind. These were part of the reason, yes, but they weren’t the complete answer. Because, there were also people I loved, who enjoyed camping and trekking together.

When I looked at it, I realized the answer was right in front of my eyes. What was I doing at 5 in the morning? Here’s another question. But this isn’t it – this is actually an answer :) At that hour, I was sitting there and thinking about it, and every time I closed my eyes, I would see the route. This was going to be my story. A story I would tell years later, recounting the events that happened to me. That’s why I wanted to go.
