We spent two days on Pokut Highland, and just as we were about to leave, we bumped into someone. They said they were heading to Verçenik Highland in Çat vadisi the next day. Hmm, Verçenik Highland sounds nice. "It's my second favourite yayla after Pokut," the friend added. I had planned to go from Pokut to Yukarı Kavrun via Ayder Highland. But these friends swayed my decision. I've been to Yukarı Kavrun many times. I said "many times" because it's more than once; I've probably been there three or four times. Çat vadisi was a place I was curious about, yet I'd never been. For some reason, I hadn't included it in my travel plans. I'd heard of Verçenik Highland before. I knew the route but had never gone. Suddenly, I made a decision and set off for Verçenik Highland. "There are crater lakes up on the highland pasture," I thought, "so I can go for a walk, and I'll pitch a tent somewhere tonight." I started planning in my head.

### Çat Valley and Verçenik Highland

After a tough, stony, and earthy path, I reached Verçenik Highland. It was around 15:00. Who goes hiking at that hour? Nobody, of course. But I trusted in my superior abilities, as if I could fly straight to the lakes and back. I had a plan to return before dark, a walk of no more than 3 hours. While I was getting ready for my hike, others were finishing theirs. We met some people. With questions like how was the trail, how long did it take, do you go often, where are you from, what is your name, your father's name, do you have a full family registry certificate, I met Osman. He was from İstanbul too. He had a house in Demirkapı village above Uzungöl in Trabzon, and yaylas higher up. He said he posted regularly in a Facebook group. They walked in groups. I didn't hold back and immediately gave Yola Çıkmalı a plug :). Saying see you around, we parted ways.

I set off at 3 o’clock and only reached the car as it got dark. Sometimes these itchy feelings take hold. I force myself to start something to get into trouble. Would it be better to stay in the dark and the fog? Anyway, when I got to the highland pasture, I thought about pitching a tent, but I was hungry and there was nowhere to get food on the highland pasture. So I decided it was best to head down to Toşi restaurant/guest house in Çat Valley and stay there. With the fantasy of eating and sleeping in a comfortable bed, I turned back towards Çat Valley.

Once you reconnect with civilisation, you immediately grab the internet. You make two posts, give three or five likes, and so on. But when you're deep in nature, you put your phone away, relax, and don't think about anything, right? Well, that's just it; man is never satisfied with anything. I log onto Facebook and immediately find Osman, and add him as a friend. He's knowledgeable and experienced. While I was thinking about benefiting from his experience, I discover he's also a member of the PasifikSpor club. His name is Osman. Eee, how is that possible? My coins start dropping. In February 2014, for the Lycia hike I was going to take in September, I had started doing cardio and bodybuilding at this gym close to home. The gym owner, Yasin abi, kept telling me, "There's someone called Osman who does trekking, always going somewhere. If we meet, I'll introduce you." Six months passed, and we never met. Then I went on the Lycia trail, came back, stopped exercising, stopped trekking, and put on some weight, yet we still hadn't met. It turns out Yasin abi was saying the same thing to Osman abi as well.

Oh, I said to myself. After so much time working together in the same gym, we never actually met, until we ran into each other spontaneously at the foot of a mountain. Some call it coincidence, others luck, and still others consider it chance. And now, I’ve finally found Osman abi.

Osman abi also returned to his village and invited me. "If you come," he said, "we can go for a walk together." Three or four days later, I arrived at Osman abi's village. We set off towards the rocky hill visible in the background of the photo (they say it's called "Kale"). With very little time, I decided to turn back halfway. There wasn't even a path. I really need to continue the trip, but I have no time—what can I do? They, however, carried on all the way to the top and even claimed to have seen a bear. Look at the opportunity we missed! As a result of a spontaneous decision, I went from knowing Osman abi only by reputation to actually meeting him at the foot of the mountain and then going for a walk together. It was a wonderful and unique memory.

If they say the world is small, then it must be.

After that, I didn’t see Osman abi again :). I don’t even know why.
