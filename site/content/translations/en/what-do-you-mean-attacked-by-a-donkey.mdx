It is the final day of our three-day Frig Valley journey (1 May 2017). We are moving through a wide area, advancing between the fields. A little further away, I saw an elderly man, a woman, and two children working in a field, while on the right, a donkey stood tethered under the shade of a tree. We were continuing our travel chat. Then I looked up and saw the donkey galloping towards us, braying and kicking up dust. At first, I didn't know what it was. Had the donkey escaped? Was there someone on it? I looked at the people in the field; they began running towards the donkey, which was rapidly approaching us. What's going on? I thought, "I should open the camera, record this moment," but there was no time. The donkey was coming fast. I spread my arms and shouted "çüşşş, hooppp, heyyy," and the rest of the team shouted too, but it made no difference. A huge donkey was coming straight at us. We were four people in the middle of the field, with nowhere to run or hide. Usually it was dogs that charged at us, and they would run off as soon as we reacted like this. But this donkey, despite all the shouting, was still coming. The field owners were also shouting. I didn't know what to do. I thought I should step aside when it arrived, but this time I might hit the others. Finally, I made a fool of myself, picked up a stone from the ground, and shouted "çüşş, hooopp" as I ran towards the donkey. It was as if the arrow had left the bow. The donkey was about to collide with us, there was no escape. Just as there was 1.5 to 2 metres between us, the donkey suddenly turned to the left and continued running along the length of the field. We felt a great relief. We had escaped the danger. I asked the owner, "What happened?" He said, "He cut the rope and ran away." "You're running away, why are you coming towards us?" I said. "There is nothing on our right or left. Run in those directions." While we were talking, the donkey was still circling the field. After a few rounds, the stupid animal grew tired. It stopped on our path. We said, "Just let it go, don't chase it, we'll love it, come here, beautiful donkey." The donkey's owner, an auntie, approached from behind and suddenly caught it by the rope. That is what the photo above shows. We loved the stupid animal a bit. The owner also said, "He couldn't go outside all winter, and now that he is outside, he has started running around." "Someone should ride it and make it run, so the animal can relax." It suddenly created excitement for us for no reason, a mad animal.
