---
title: "A Phone Call from the Kaçkar Mountains"
description: "I’d been emailing the national parks about fees, litter and entry rules. Two years later, replies started arriving. Then the phone rang."
---
7 January 2017

After every visit to the national parks, my outrage grew even more. And on every return from a trip, I had written complaint emails to the national parks. Different pricing schedules, the arbitrary setting of fees by whoever collected them, the filth of the parks, the ban on entry without a site guide, etc. These are just the problems I can recall. In fact, I can't even remember how many emails I sent or what I wrote in them. Last week, I noticed that emails began coming to me from various national parks under the forestry authority in Türkiye. Receiving four emails at once startled me considerably. About two years had passed. It was good to get a reply, even if it came late, but I couldn't recall what I had complained about regarding which national park. The emails, which began with "Regarding Practices in National Parks," didn't state what I had complained about, but they did contain explanations relevant to the matter. Yesterday, after receiving an email from Kaçkar Dağları Milli Park, my phone rang. Yes, the call was from Kaçkar. They informed me that the emails I had written had been forwarded to them by the forest management. Although I couldn't quite recall what I had complained about in my 2015 email, the person on the phone told me that he had been there for eight months and had examined the subject matter. He stated that he had sent a clarifying email and would follow up on the issues I had raised. I still couldn't remember what I had complained about. However, it made me very glad to know that my complaint had been taken into account by someone. The fact that Ahmet Bey was thinking of coming to Kaçkar, and if I came, we could go out to the field together, made me even happier. The Eastern Black Sea region, the Kaçkar Mountains, is a region I admire. Why not? :)

A bit about what the emails said. They sent regulations covering site guides, cleanliness and operating practices. The people charging arbitrary fees had been warned, and I was asked to report it immediately if it happened again. The national parks regulation, whichever national park you visit, says: “A 50% discount applies outside the period from 1 June to 15 September.” Check that you’re charged the tariff for the dates of your visit.

Receiving a response after two years regarding my complaints made me happy. I don’t know whether any action will be taken, or not. However, my complaint has been acknowledged, and a reply has been provided. If I see it again, I will complain again. You should also look for it. You can find information on the website of the national parks. I’m not adding a link, in case it changes.
