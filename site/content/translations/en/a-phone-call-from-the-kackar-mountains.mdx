7 January 2017

After each visit to the national parks, my irritation grew even more. And on every return from a trip, I had written complaint emails to the national parks. These included different pricing schedules, the arbitrary setting of fees by those collecting them, the unhygienic condition of the parks, the prohibition of entry without a site plan, and so on. These are merely the problems I can recall. In fact, I cannot even remember how many emails I sent or what I wrote in them. Last week, I noticed that emails began coming to me from various national parks under the forestry authority in Türkiye. Receiving four emails at once startled me considerably. Approximately two years had passed since then. It is always nice to get a reply, even if it comes late, but I cannot recall what I had complained about regarding which national park. The emails, which began with "Practices in National Parks," did not state what I had complained about, but they did contain explanations relevant to the matter. Yesterday, after receiving an email from the Kaçkar Dağları Milli Park, my phone rang. Yes, the call was from Kaçkar. They informed me that the emails I had written had been forwarded to them by the forest management. Although I could not quite recall what I had complained about in my 2015 email, the person on the phone told me that he had been there for eight months and had examined the subject matter. He stated that he had sent a clarifying email and would follow up on the issues I had raised. I still could not remember what I had complained about. However, it made me very glad to know that my complaint had been taken into account by someone. Ahmet asked, "Are you thinking of visiting the Kaçkar? If you come, we can go out together to the site," which made me quite happy. The eastern Black Sea region and the Kaçkar Mountains are a region I admire greatly. Why not? :)

A bit about what the emails said. They sent regulations covering site guides, cleanliness and operating practices. The people charging arbitrary fees had been warned, and I was asked to report it immediately if it happened again. The national parks regulation, whichever national park you visit, says: “A 50% discount applies outside the period from 1 June to 15 September.” Check that you’re charged the tariff for the dates of your visit.

Receiving a response after two years regarding my complaints made me happy. I don’t know whether any action will be taken, or not. However, my complaint has been acknowledged, and a reply has been provided. If I see it again, I will complain again. You should also look for it. You can find information on the website of the national parks. I’m not adding a link, in case it changes.
