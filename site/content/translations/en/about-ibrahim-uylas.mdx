description: "I’m a software engineer who loves camping, trekking and riding a motorcycle. Here’s where I’ve been, what I’ve learned and what I dream of doing next."
---

Hmm, actually it doesn’t matter, not at all. In the face of the universe’s vastness, who is İbrahim? Of course, what I am and who I am make no difference. I’m just a traveller passing by, so insignificant I’m not even a tiny detail. But I am the king of my own mind. When it’s just me, the boss is always me. I like myself, I love myself, and I praise myself. And I do that only when I’m alone. I don’t let anyone else have that ;). Born and raised in Erzurum (18), studied at university in Cyprus (5), wrote code as a white-collar worker in İstanbul (8), then moved to London (4). For now, I’m a dirty immigrant and, at the same time, an expat. 18 from there, 5 from here, 8 from İstanbul and 4 from London. What’s the total? Thirty-five. What do we say? Hay maşallah, ajsdhasgsdsfg.

I’m a computer engineer and I work in software. Four years ago I came all the way to London to travel the world and work. Money doesn’t just flow in from somewhere, so I glue myself to the keyboard five days a week and tell myself to get to work if I want to chase my dreams. I earn my own money and travel under my own sponsorship. When there’s no work, I head outdoors. I like living well and living well (what does living well mean? A good question). Staying positive is my closest friend. Any place I can explore freely and is worth seeing is also worth dreaming about. Camping, listening to my own soul and to nature’s soul when I’m alone outdoors, hiking and trekking, and riding a motorcycle are the activities I love most in this life.

I’m fiercely attached to my freedom and my dreams. I’m always dreaming. As soon as one dream comes true, I go after the next. That’s how my life goes. — Mad, probably. No wife, no children. He won’t even own a single stone as he chases his dreams, not noticing the whistle.

Living in the moment and enjoying it is very much me. The past is over. The future hasn’t arrived yet, and it’s beautiful because that’s where my dreams are. Life is happening right now. So I take my coffee, sit by a lake and stare at nature like an idiot for half an hour. It may look that way to somebody else, but I’m looking at the details. I watch the wind, the forest, the birds, the movement on the lake and the sunlight. I picture my dreams. I feel the outdoors around me. Every time I travel alone, I find myself questioning life, existence, humanity, beliefs, history, the universe and myself. I ask myself questions and look for answers. I haven’t got anywhere yet. I’ll write about it if I do :)

I’m no Instagrammer, YouTuber, influencer or blogger. I just like being here and sharing the good things I capture. Maybe one day I’ll be the guy who makes little documentaries. Who knows what time will bring? I don’t call myself a traveller for a living either. I don’t do any of this for work, and I wouldn’t take it up as a job or do it to make money. I love my profession and plan to keep living like this. I’m not leaving my job to open a café. That’s somebody else’s dream.

In the short term, my dreams are as follows: to build a wooden canoe, cross the 90 km Caledonian Canal in Scotland in five days during the summer of 2022, and then hang around there for a while, floating my handmade canoe on the lochs, watching the sun set and rise. Also, if there's an opportunity due to COVID, I might tour the Scandinavian countries by motorbike. I tried the 'work and travel' model this summer by [staying in Scotland for a month](/tek-basina-iskoc-yaylalarinda-29-gunde-5500km/). It works like a charm.

My longer-term plans aren’t far off. If I get British citizenship in about two and a half years, that’s when seeing the world really begins for me. Before that, I dream of taking six months and riding a motorcycle to the Far East. Leaving work and everything else behind, jumping on the bike in London and riding to the far side of the world. Once I have citizenship, I want to buy a four-wheel drive, kit it out with a tent, awning and the rest, and head out into the world. Wherever the vehicle takes me and my British or Turkish passport opens a door, that place will be home. Switzerland and the Alps, Canada and North America are among the places I most want to see. I even have a plan to spend a few years working remotely over there, earning pounds and exploring. Sounds great to me. If I ever have to settle in one place, I’d choose to stay here in the UK. I can almost hear people calling me a foreigner now. I don’t particularly care. What am I supposed to do, my friend, move back to Erzurum? ;)

My outlook on life is extremely friendly and positive. I’m not entirely sure where the friendly bit came from. I just made it up. Life is well worth living. I know there’s plenty of awful stuff in the world, but we ought to spend more time looking at what’s good. After all, we’re the ones making the mess, us insatiable human beings.

If you opened this page and kept reading this far, thank you. You gave me some of your valuable time, and I know what that’s worth. When I write, I try to put something of myself and my life into it and offer a few different ideas and ways of looking at things. I try to pass on what I’ve learned, the way others have done for me. Setting off and being on the road mean more to me than getting from place to place. Each journey has its own depth and meaning. Nine years ago, someone older than me said, “Don’t worry. Water flows and finds its own way,” with no idea how much those words would shape my life. That’s life. Someone touches yours, and you touch someone else’s. I hope we always leave something good behind.

Take care.
