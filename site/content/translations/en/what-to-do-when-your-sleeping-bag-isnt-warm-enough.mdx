It’s happened to me plenty of times. You’ve probably had nights like that too, shivering because your sleeping bag just wasn’t warm enough. It’s amazing what you come up with when you’re stuck. You or I could sit here and think about it all day and get nowhere. Put us in that cold bag and the little light bulbs start coming on, one by one.

## What can you do if your sleeping bag isn’t warm enough at camp?

* If there’s a car nearby, you’re in luck. Go and lie down immediately. Also, run the interior heating occasionally. You’ll sleep wonderfully; there’s nothing better.
* If there’s no car, putting on very thick clothes can work against you. The sleeping bag holds in the air your body has warmed. Instead of wearing all the extra layers, try draping spare clothes over the bag like a blanket.
* Focus on things you can do to insulate the ground. If you don’t have a good mat, you can use a bag, a sack, or whatever you find – but you need to insulate the ground. The most effective method is to spread an aluminum blanket you find in a first aid kit on the ground. If the ground insulation is good, you can wrap a sleeping bag around it. Aluminum blankets are lightweight and take up little space. Having them with you is very beneficial.
* There are adhesive heaters available. If you apply one to your foot and another to your lower back, they can provide approximately at least 12 hours of warmth. Then, a really good night's sleep. It’s been proven.
* If there’s no stick heater, then use a hot water bag. Don’t even say “Where am I supposed to find this at the top of the mountain.” Fill a 500ml pet bottle with water and heat it over a fire (don’t worry, when you hold the full water bottle over the fire, it won’t immediately burn). If you put it inside the sleeping bag, it will act like a warm stove.
* If you run a short loop before getting into your sleeping bag, your body temperature will increase. This will allow you to heat the air inside the sleeping bag more quickly.
* It’s coldest around 4–5 a.m., shortly before sunrise. Keep that in mind. Make it through that hour and you’ll live a little longer.
* A method to try and heat the air inside a sleeping bag with your breath. However, due to the moisture in your breath, the inside gets wet and won't heat up, which will make you cold. Not recommended. Do not use.
* If you’re really in a tight spot, don’t sleep. Get up, light a fire, make tea, make soup, and warm up properly. Try to avoid getting pneumonia. If there’s a risk of local frostbite events, such as your fingers or toes freezing, then don’t sleep. I hope you never experience such things.
