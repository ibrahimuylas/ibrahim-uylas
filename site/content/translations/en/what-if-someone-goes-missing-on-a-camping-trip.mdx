Has there ever been a time when a friend disappeared at the campsite? Have you ever gotten lost at a campsite? If so, what did you feel? This time’s story is about a friend who got lost at the campsite.

After an approximately 4-5 hour hike, we entered our tents to rest upon returning to the camping area. The weather was rainy and foggy. The rain would start, then stop. The fog was also uneven. It was hard to tell what the weather was doing. After lighting a fire to relax, I went into the tent after resting for about half an hour. After about an hour, we aimed to prepare food and chat by the fire. Around 6:15 PM, Emre and Ramazan took action and began to build up the fire. Then a voice called out, “Sinan, where are you?” When I entered the tent, I saw he had also come out and gone to a place in the back. But he had been gone for a while. “There’s something strange about him not being around for so long,” I said. We searched the surrounding area, considering possibilities like he might have needed to use the toilet or perhaps fallen asleep. But he was still nowhere to be found. Around 7 PM. After an hour of searching the surrounding area, we couldn't find Sinan. “It’s possible that some wild animal appeared and he panicked and ran into the forest,” we added. There’s nothing else that could have happened, or anywhere he could have gone, because he was only wearing a shirt in this rainy, cool weather. The weather was about to become dark within approximately half an hour, and Sinan was still missing. The phones weren’t receiving signal. We are in Menekşe Subatımı’s valley in Kocaeli. “There’s no one to ask for help,” I said. “Because he hasn’t been around for two hours, we’ve decided to take the matter up with the authorities, specifically the Jandarma.” There’s a place with a phone signal – approximately 2 meters in diameter – in the flat area between Menekşe Subatımı’s valley and Subatımı valley. We discovered this place during a hike the day before.

If you were to ask us, we would not allow you to go there.

I drove towards this point, sounding the car horn as if I were part of a wedding convoy. I hoped that, if anyone nearby heard the car, they would come. Of course, hope is the bread of the poor. First, I called 112 and told them our location and that my friend had gone missing. The response was surprising: "This is Bursa, you are in Kocaeli; you should have called them." As if I had dialed the area code before calling 112. I don't know, buddy, the line seemed to have been routed there. Then, I left my phone number and waited for them to call. The Jandarma called, and I explained the same situation to the on-duty commander. His comment was also quite interesting: "Why did you go there to set up a camp? If you had asked us, we would not have allowed you to go there." Okay, we should have obtained permission from the Jandarma, but our friend is nowhere to be found, and that does not change the situation. After explaining to the commander that our friend was not a mischievous person, and that he could even be afraid of a lizard, and that he would not have started such an undertaking to scare us, the commander said he would find the muhtar of the village to which he was attached and come together with him. He asked us to go to the campsite and wait for them. The weather was already dark, and it was raining heavily. Sinan is nowhere to be found. While I was waiting for Akut to arrive and requesting their assistance, the commander told us that we needed to circulate in the area by announcing ourselves, in order for them to arrive. This problem is because it will take 10 hours for them to arrive. After 1–2 hours of searching without news, he will ask for Akut's assistance. They will arrive at best between 3 and 4 in the morning. If something has happened to Sinan, it becomes increasingly important with every passing minute.

Trying to preserve our strength, we decided to return to the camp site, and also to go to the other valley to ask for help, or at least gather some information. Because we were now in a panic, and scenarios about how to find Sinan were running through our minds – is he dead or alive? Yes, we were in such a bad situation, and as time passed, our mental state deteriorated. One of the valley elders told me that he had seen that young man. He was here around seven, walking, and he was camping in the other valley. These details match Sinan. But even though 1.5 hours had passed, the fact that he had not yet returned shows that something had happened to him. Upon hearing that Sinan had set off on his own, without telling anyone, Ramazan and I were suddenly very upset. We went from anger to madness. But at the same time, we felt a glimmer of hope – as if we would find him. We immediately jumped into the car and set off on the road between the two valleys to search. At least we knew where to look. As we were driving along the road (kornaya devam), we saw a light suddenly turn towards us. Ohhhh, we found Sinan. He was soaked through, and his clothes were stained. We were about to go after him when we found him – he was covered in mud. We were so overjoyed at finding him that we completely forgot about hurting him.

Sinan was feeling down, and decided to walk to the other valley without saying anything, in order not to bother us. On the way back, he encountered some rogues and, frightened, ran into the forest. Then, he lost his way. After sitting under a tree and gathering his strength, he noticed a lantern had accidentally appeared in his pocket, and with renewed courage, he set off. It was thanks to his exploration of the area the day before, describing which path he had taken, that brought Sinan back to us.

Ultimately, it was a situation where we were both trying to get cheap results and were very worried. Even just considering the possibility of finding a close friend – alive or dead – was quite unpleasant. Yes, you as you, we as we, let’s all be ourselves and be careful not to do anything without letting anyone know. You might say, “But you’re going out alone.” But I’ve never, and I still try not, to put my safety at risk :-).
