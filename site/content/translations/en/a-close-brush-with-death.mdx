description: "In the summer of 2016, on a Saturday morning, I packed my belongings and loaded them onto my motorcycle."

In the summer of 2016, on a Saturday morning, I packed my belongings and loaded them onto my motorcycle. Then, I set off. My destination was the Sadağı canyon in Bursa Orhaneli. I planned to ride from İstanbul there, and then take a day trip, with the intention of visiting my aunts in Bursa.

My soul is wild, but in reality, I am not a reckless motorcyclist. I generally do not drive too fast or take unnecessary risks; I usually go gently. Strangely, on that day, I was riding quite aggressively – there were moments where I cut in and moments where I slammed the throttle, and so on. When I reached the Gemlik area, there is a winding road leading out of Gemlik towards Bursa. After climbing the hill, you reach a straight road leading to Bursa. It was just as I approached that final straight section, driving on the far left of the three-lane road, that a devilish thing happened. I am in the leftmost lane and there is a curve to the right. I looked and the right lanes were empty. The devil whispered to me: "If you push towards the rightmost lane, you will take the curve more easily, and if you think that way, the car and the truck will have already passed." I thought about it, and it seemed very logical. I crossed over into the rightmost lane, tilted the motorcycle to the right to take the curve, and as I was exiting the curve, what do you know, my lane was flashing. As I was accelerating to exit the curve, at the same time with the ground at a 60-degree angle, I found myself on the oil on the road. I did not escape anywhere. In fact, the entire lane was covered in oil, so where could I escape? At that moment, time stopped for me. Everything stopped. While the devil was whispering, Azrail was saying "come, come". In my last breath, profanities were coming out of my mouth. Not against anyone, I was just saying it out loud. What could I do, how could I calculate how to break it, when the rear wheel slipped, and because I did not fully correct the motorcycle, I could not escape. My speed was around 80, and the rear wheel slid to the right, then to the left, and we continued like that for a while. At that moment, I was trying to regain control by alternating between giving it gas and braking, hoping to control it. Of course, it was in vain. The last thing I remember is that we were parallel to a lane tilted to the left, yes, with the motorcycle and me. If I did not let go, the motorcycle would have thrown me off, and there would be no salvation. At that moment, it was as if I had passed Yusuf and Yusuf. My last breath. I could not even say, "I am coming to You, God." In fact, the incident happened within 2-3 seconds, at most 5 seconds. In such situations, they say, "get off the motorcycle so that it does not throw you off and chase you." I have this theoretical knowledge. At that moment, I decided to practice that theory immediately and let go of the motorcycle. I thought, "we are going ahead, I will follow from behind, maybe I will catch up," but the motorcycle was moving faster.

When I suddenly fell to the ground, I didn’t understand what happened. I looked around, wondering if I was rolling, if I had hit something, or if I was still myself, and I noticed that the sky looked incredibly blue. At the same time, I heard a song by Bob Dylan playing in my ears, and I was sliding across the ground. When I realized I was sliding on my back, I quickly gathered my hands and feet. It was as if I had instinctively pulled my head back. If I hadn't pulled my head back, I would have hit the ground with a “tak tak tak” sound, and then my neck would have broken, and I probably wouldn't have been able to write this.

When I stopped sliding, I didn’t get up straight away because I couldn’t tell what was wrong with me. I lifted my head a little and there was a huge lorry coming straight at me. “Oh, shit,” for the second time. I slid myself over into the hard shoulder. I’d survived the crash only to end up under a lorry. Could you believe it? Luckily the driver saw me fall and stopped close to me so cars wouldn’t suddenly pass him on the left and run me over. He ran over right away, bless him. I checked my hands, my feet and then very gently my neck. Nothing. How could that be? I’d expected some kind of injury after that fall, but I seemed right as rain. Or thought I was. Once I knew my neck was all right, I took off my helmet and stood up. I really was fine. I ached left and right from the fall, but I was OK. Thank goodness.

I examined the motor – there was no motor. It had rolled a bit to the side and into the grass. I figured, “Let it be here,” – it was definitely broken, and there was no need to take it. I decided it was enough to save my life. I went down to see what was inside the motor. And there was nothing there either. Just some protective bars were tangled. A man helped me move the motor to the emergency lane, and he continued, so as not to cause another accident. While I was carefully checking the motor, about 20 minutes had passed since the accident, and an ambulance arrived. “There’s an accident here,” they said, “we didn’t see it, it was hidden.” A doctor, who examined the scene with his eyes, saw nothing and completed the ambulance record and continued on his way. At that time, a neighbor passing by scolded me because I was riding the motor. “Why was the neighbor on the Bursa – İstanbul highway? Where was the neighbor coming from? Where was she going? There weren’t any settlements nearby either, and I still don’t understand.” Despite having been given the warning to not ride this motor, a pickup truck stopped, and a friendly motor-riding friend helped me. After checking the motor and feeling better, I told him that I would continue after a short rest, and I said goodbye to him.

I thought I’d take another look at the motor, to see if there was anything missing or anything I hadn’t noticed. I realized there was something there that I couldn’t see. The protective bars were crooked, my mirrors were broken, and the attachment point for the rear bag was detached. That’s it, nothing else. I honestly don’t understand how this happened. If they had told me to try to avoid an accident while going at 80 km/h, but to ensure that neither I nor the motor were harmed, this is the most likely outcome. I wondered if the blessings of my elders were somehow upon me, but thank God, I arrived in Bursa from the safety lane after about half an hour. There were only about 10 km left, so it wasn’t a major problem. I found a mechanic and had the motor checked, and of course, I cancelled the hike. I was practically dead an hour ago, and now I’m cancelling the hike. I celebrated because I managed to achieve the example of avoiding an accident, so that neither we nor the motor were damaged.

Ultimately, even if you are always careful and aware of your surroundings, you can’t always predict what will happen. Thankfully, I managed to avoid a serious accident and realized once again the importance of the equipment I was wearing.

May our journeys be safe and without incident, and may our steps remain light.
