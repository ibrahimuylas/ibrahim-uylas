This time, we had wanted to camp in a place near İstanbul. When I looked at the map, I saw that there were forested areas to set up a tent near Gebze Denizli Lake. It was just one hour away, right on the lake shore, and in a forested area. We just wondered what else we could want. Because it was close to İstanbul, the possibility of the lake shore being crowded worried us, but we decided, “let’s just go a little further into the forest.” We got in our cars on Saturday morning and headed off. There were a few people around the lake, and it felt quite peaceful. However, we couldn't find an isolated and beautiful spot to set up a tent. We thought that these areas would get busy on Sunday, so we continued a bit further towards the Black Sea, into the forest. Despite the risk of scraping the bottom of our car in a few places due to the rough road, we found a beautiful spot in the forest. There wasn't anyone around. “Perfect!” we exclaimed, and we set up our campfire. We enjoyed a wonderful time, with complete darkness, full stomachs, and the pleasant warmth of the fire on our faces.

### A ghost story by the campfire

As the hours passed, Aydan started telling us a horror story. There was a little girl cutting her nails in the bathroom. Then her aunt came over. The girl asked her aunt, "Auntie, do witches' feet point backwards, is that true?" Her aunt showed her own feet and said, "Like this?" Just as Aydan finished her sentence, we heard a scream from inside the forest: "İsmaaiiilll!" Yes, this ridiculous tale was nothing to be scared of or laugh at, but where had that Ismail sound come from? We all looked at each other, terrified, saying "What's going on, man?" while the woman shouted "İsmaaaiiilll!" again. We looked around; it was pitch black everywhere. The sound seemed to be coming from the opposite hill. But there was no light, no nothing. Either the woman was lost or Ismail was lost. I was getting the radios and flashlights ready, saying "Let's take a look," when what do I see? The two Merve in our team were running off towards where the sound came from. They hadn't taken flashlights or anything. They only had wine glasses. "Are you going to do first aid with wine? :)" We shouted and stopped them. Then, after getting the radios and flashlights ready, we headed up the hill together. After the Ismail scream had come 2–3 times, it stopped. We climbed the hill, wandered a bit in the forest, looked around, called out, and shone our lights everywhere, but there were no spirits. No car driving away either. We were all quite uneasy. Did someone get lost? Was someone strangling another? We didn't know. We didn't even catch where the sound went, and we returned to the campsite. Maybe the sound was an echo coming from the hill. That's possible too. Sounds spread in the forest; you can't tell where they come from. We couldn't pin this down to anything. It was a night where a woman shouting "İsmail" in the middle of the night gave us the jitters. We still don't know what happened to Ismail.

### What should you do if you hear someone scream while you’re camping?

Moral of the story: no ghost stories around the campfire. Joking aside, if you hear a scream or someone calling out near camp, get yourself somewhere safe first. Try to work out why they’re shouting. If someone’s in danger and asking for help, call the gendarmerie straight away (156 when I wrote this, now 112 in Türkiye). From a distance you can call back and shine a torch. An attacker could turn on you too, especially if they’re armed. If it’s someone who’s lost, your light can help them find you. It’s harder to get your bearings in the woods at night, so don’t go wandering far. If you lose track of where you are, you could get lost trying to reach them.
