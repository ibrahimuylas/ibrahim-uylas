This time, we had wanted to camp in a place near İstanbul. When I looked at the map, I saw that there were forested areas to set up a tent near Gebze Denizli Lake. It was just one hour away, right on the lake shore, and in a forested area. We just wondered what else we could want. Because it was close to İstanbul, the possibility of the lake shore being crowded worried us, but we decided, “let’s just go a little further into the forest.” We got in our cars on Saturday morning and headed off. There were a few people around the lake, and it felt quite peaceful. However, we couldn't find an isolated and beautiful spot to set up a tent. We thought that these areas would get busy on Sunday, so we continued a bit further towards the Black Sea, into the forest. Despite the risk of scraping the bottom of our car in a few places due to the rough road, we found a beautiful spot in the forest. There wasn't anyone around. “Perfect!” we exclaimed, and we set up our campfire. We enjoyed a wonderful time, with complete darkness, full stomachs, and the pleasant warmth of the fire on our faces.

### KAMPTA – A TALE OF FEAR

As the hours passed, Aydan began to tell us a frightening story. There was a little girl who was cutting her nails in the bath. Then her aunt came over and asked her, "Is it true that your aunt's feet are facing backwards?" Aydan confirmed this. Her aunt then showed her feet, saying, "Yes, like this." Just as Aydan was finishing her sentence, we heard a scream from within the forest: "Ismail." Yes, this ridiculous tale was nothing to be scared of or laughed at, but where had that Ismail sound come from? We all looked at each other, saying, "What's going on?" The woman then shouted "Ismail" again. We looked around; it was pitch black everywhere. The sound seemed to be coming from the far side. But there was no light. Either the woman was lost, or Ismail was lost. I was preparing the radios and flashlights, saying, "Let's look around," when I saw the 2 people named Merve in our group running towards where the sound was coming from. They had found nothing with the flashlights – just wine glasses. "Are you going to treat them with wine?" we asked. We stopped them and, after preparing the radios and flashlights, we went up the hill together. After the voice had screamed İsmail’s name 2–3 times, it fell silent. We climbed the hill, wandered a bit in the forest, looked around, called out, and directed our lights, but there were no demons. There was no car or anyone driving away. We were all quite uneasy. We didn't know if someone was lost, or if someone was being harmed, we couldn't figure out where the sound had gone. We couldn't connect this to anything; it was a night where a woman kept shouting "Ismail." And we still don't know what happened to Ismail.

### If you detect a Kampta field issue, what should you do?

Ultimately, don’t tell scary stories around the campsite. Aside from the joke, if you hear a scream/call/shout at the campsite, your first action should be to ensure your own safety. Then, try to understand why the person was shouting. If someone is in danger and needs help, immediately contact the police (156). If the sound is coming from a distant location, shine a flashlight and try to help them find the person. It’s more difficult to find your way in the forest at night. Therefore, don’t wander too far or for too long. If you don’t know where you are, you could get lost while trying to reach someone.
