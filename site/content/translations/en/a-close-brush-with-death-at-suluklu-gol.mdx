After a lovely morning breakfast, we packed our bags and left the car near Sülüklü Lake, and we began our trek. Our goal was to reach the lake, which was approximately 15km away. We needed to make a “hilal” – a crescent shape – from the beginning of the path to reach the top. This was our route. As we were starting, Furkan and Gürkan began to say, “It would have been much closer if we had started from the lake’s slope,” complaining that you were getting tired during the trek. “I say it in my head, not to anyone, so no one’s mood should drop. :) And, these things we were saying at that moment were just a result of the difficulties we were experiencing. It’s a clever trick, really. You can get into trouble and say a lot, and still enjoy yourself.”

On the first day, we couldn’t complete 15km, and we didn’t reach the lake. Therefore, we set up camp in a small, wooded spot along the road, and the next morning we began our return journey with a meager breakfast. Everyone was exhausted and just wanting to get back to the car. Then, we climbed the hill and began the descent. We had to use the “hilal” tactic again, and thankfully, this time we were descending. Suddenly, Furkan and Gürkan brought up the topic they had been discussing in secret. “Let’s go from the left side. We can descend from the slope – it will be closer and faster.” I, of course, was quite surprised – how did I not think of this :), but definitely not. Look, this is an unknown road – it slopes down to a cliff, with bushes blocking the way, making it impossible and unsafe to go through and descend. I said, “But we’re descending this road, so let’s return along it,” but it was useless; they kept giving me a ‘you’re being too smart’ look. “It’ll be okay, let’s have an adventure – it will be more fun,” they insisted. “I said, ‘But I had a solution in mind – once we descend into the valley, we’ll try to get across to the other side and descend from that road.’” Because we had a solution, I said, “Let’s descend towards the valley, which is covered in forest.”

Our start was pleasant; the forest wasn't too dense and, since the slope wasn't too steep, we descended easily. As we said, the weather was good. Then, the bushes began to thicken and the slope started to increase. We continued by finding transition points from right to left, from the coast to the corner. Just 20 metres before descending into the valley, our packs were getting snagged on the brambles, and the slope had become so steep that we were descending from tree to tree and from grass to grass. Upon reaching the valley, with the thought that the rest would be easy and we could proceed without trouble, we also passed this 20-metre section. Hooopp, and what do we see? A stream was flowing between the two slopes. Of course, it shouldn't have been. Generally, that's how it is, so why couldn't you have thought of it? I didn't know then, and at that moment I hadn't been thinking about it. If it had occurred to me, we wouldn't have gone in. There was nothing we could do; since we couldn't return, we had to continue. The low water level of the stream was a great relief for us. We began to progress nicely, as if we were going to reach Sülüklü Lake within an hour. Then, another surprise. A waterfall at a height of 10 metres. We found a spot where we could pass with half steps and managed to get through that as well. The place we were in was truly beautiful and untouched, but it wasn't suitable for entering with our backpacks. There was practically nothing suitable, I think, it would be madness :) – next time, come back and take a walk through the stream, it would be super.

With all these nice thoughts going through our heads, we reached somewhere even worse. This time the drop was about 20 metres. Great. We were about to run out of patience. I went ahead to look for a way across and told my brother Furkan to follow slowly. The weak grass he’d grabbed tore out and he started sliding down. I could see him falling and there was absolutely nothing I could do. He was too far away for me to catch. I waited helplessly to see what would happen. He grabbed a few solid branches and stopped. It took about 2 seconds but felt like 2 hours. We breathed again. I remember Gürkan saying, “I’m scared of heights. I can’t get across here” :). Furkan hadn’t even realised how high up we were or where he was falling. If he’d gone over, there was nothing we could do. Death had just brushed past him. “Come on, we’re going back,” I said, and we returned to the stream.

Now the pieces were in my hands. My righteousness had become apparent. But what am I supposed to do, trying to be right in such a ridiculous place? As I was wondering whether to go back and what we would do, my followers obeyed me, and they tied themselves to me with a firm adherence, saying, “We won’t argue, we’ll just do as you say.” I wished I could have tied myself to someone at that moment, someone who could tell me to get out of here. At that time, Gürkan had a brilliant idea: “Let’s call AFAD, they’ll save us.” Of course, AFAD arrives in one day, and then they beat us here, asking us, “What are you doing here?”

There’s nothing to do. “I’ll implement my alternative solution,” I said. And we managed to reach the top, crawling on the ground for about 250 meters. It was really a long crawl. We took a break every 5 meters when we stopped, resting by wrapping ourselves around the trunks of the trees. At one point, we even thought we wouldn’t be able to get out. Then we reached the path, and after a few hours, we arrived at the lake near the car.

If time were to pass normally, we would arrive approximately two hours earlier and be less tired. And, most importantly, we were putting our lives at risk. Furkan was moving between two worlds.

So, what it means is that you won’t embark on a path you don’t know, you won’t hurt people’s hearts, you won’t risk your life, and you won’t engage in a struggle against nature. In essence, you will be a gentleman.
