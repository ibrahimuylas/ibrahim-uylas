---
title: "A Close Brush with Death at Sülüklü Göl"
description: "My brother Furkan started sliding, and I was too far away to catch him. This is what happened when we tried a shortcut back to Sülüklü Göl."
---

After a good breakfast we packed up, left the car by Sülüklü Göl and set off for another lake about 15 km away. Our route swung round in a big crescent to gain the ridge. We’d barely started when Furkan and Gürkan pointed to the slope by the lake. “We should’ve gone up there. It’s shorter.” Mate, you’ve come hiking and you’re complaining about the walking? I do my share of swearing when the going gets tough, but I keep it in my head. No need to bring the whole team down :). Besides, the grumbling only lasts as long as the climb. That’s the strange pleasure of it. You get yourself into trouble, curse every minute and somehow still enjoy it. What sort of madness is that?

We didn’t manage the 15 km or make it to the lake that day. So we set up camp in a lightly wooded spot beside the road and, the next morning, set off back with a poor breakfast. Our lot were fed up. They were already calculating how to get to the car as soon as possible. Then we reached the top and were about to start the descent. Again, we needed to use the crescent tactic, and the good thing was that this time we were going downhill. Suddenly, Furkan and Gürkan brought up the subject they’d been discussing secretly between themselves. “Let’s go that way on the left. We’ll go down the slope; it’ll be closer and quicker.” Of course, I feigned surprise: “Aaa, how did I not think of that? :)” No, of course not. Look, that’s an unknown path; it could be a slope, a cliff, scrub blocking the way—we couldn’t get through, couldn’t go, and it wouldn’t be safe. I said we should go down and return the way we came, but it was futile. They gave me that “know-it-all” look. “It’ll be fine, we’ll have an adventure, it’ll be more fun,” they insisted, insisted. To stop them thinking I was digging my heels in, I said yes. But I’d already decided: if we couldn’t continue down the valley, we’d get to the other side and go down from there. Since we had an alternative, I said, “Let’s try it,” and we started down into the forested valley.

Our entry was good; the forest wasn't too dense and the slope wasn't too steep, so we were descending comfortably. Those of us had told you, they were in good spirits. Then the bushes began to thicken and the slope to steepen. We carried on finding passages from right and left, from the shore and the corners. With just 20 metres to go before entering the valley, our packs were already snagging on the scrub, and the slope had become so steep that we were descending by holding onto trees and grass. Thinking it would be easy once we reached the valley and we could continue comfortably, we got past that 20 metres. Hooopp, what do we see? A stream flowing between two slopes. Of course, what else would flow? It's usually like that, why couldn't you have thought of it? I couldn't have guessed, I hadn't thought of that at all. If that had occurred to me, we'd never have entered. There's nothing to be done; since we can't turn back, we have to carry on. The fact that the stream's water was low was a great relief for us. We started making good progress. It felt as if we'd reach Sülüklü Göl within an hour. Then another surprise: a ten-metre waterfall. We found a way through with half-steps and got past it. The place we were in was truly beautiful and very untouched, but it's not suitable for entering with backpacks. Not suitable? It's not even possible, I think it's absolute madness :). It would be brilliant to come another time and walk inside the stream.

With all these nice thoughts, we reached a place even worse. This time the drop was about 20 metres. Oh no. Our patience was about to snap. I looked ahead to see if there was a way across, then told Furkan (he could be my brother) to follow me slowly. Suddenly, the weak grass he’d grabbed tore out and he started sliding down. The kid was flying down right before my eyes, and I could do absolutely nothing. There was too much distance between us for me to catch him. Helplessly, I waited to see what would happen. He grabbed a few solid branches and stopped. It took about 2 seconds, but it felt like 2 hours to me. We let out a deep breath. At that moment, I remembered Gürkan saying, “Bro, I have a fear of heights; I can’t get across here” :). Furkan hadn’t even realised how high up we were or where he’d fall. The kid was gone, so there was nothing we could do. Azrail had just brushed past Furkan. “Let’s go back,” I said, and we returned to the stream.

Well, now I held all the cards. I’d been right. A fat lot of good that did me with a steep slope on either side. How were we getting out? Should we turn back? My two disciples had suddenly found their faith. “Whatever you say. Tell us black is white and we won’t argue.” I’d have loved someone to follow just then, someone I could ask to get me out of there. Gürkan had a bright idea. “Let’s ring AFAD. They’ll rescue us.” Sure, the rescue team would get there in a day, and then they’d give us a right hiding for getting ourselves in there.

There was nothing else to do. I said, "We'll use my alternative solution." And we managed to reach the top, crawling about 250 metres, holding onto the grass. There's no exaggeration here; we really crawled on the ground. Every five metres we took a break, and as soon as we stopped, we hugged a tree trunk to rest. What's more, the scrub was so thick that for a moment the thought of not climbing out even crossed my mind. Then we reached that path, and a few hours later we arrived back at the lake beside the car.

If we’d taken the usual route, we’d have been back about two hours earlier and a lot less tired. More to the point, we’d risked our lives. Furkan had already had one foot in the next world.

What did I take away from that? Stay off paths you don’t know. Better to hurt someone’s feelings than let them crack their head open. Don’t gamble with your life or pick a fight with nature. In short, behave yourself.
