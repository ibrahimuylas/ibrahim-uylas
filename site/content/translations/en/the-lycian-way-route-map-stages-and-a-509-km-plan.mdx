This personal route plan details the 509 km Lycian Way route I prepared for my 2014 hike, including its stages, distances, water points, and accommodation notes.

## Quick summary

- Planned duration: 16 days of hiking and 3 days of rest.
- **509 km:** The total length of the Lycian Way in my 2014 plan when alternative branches are included.
- 465 km: This is the total of the 16 planned hiking days, which I’ve calculated on paper, by choosing from these options.
- **430 km:** The single-track GPS record from Ölüdeniz to Hisarçandır, reproduced below.
- 230 km: Walked a real 11-day stretch between Ölüdeniz and Kaleüçağız, between 20 and 30 September 2014.
- **Period:** September–October 2014
- Scope: Daily distance, altitude, water, and accommodation notes

> Update note: This plan is based on preparations made in 2014. Trail markers, water sources, businesses, prices, and crossing conditions may vary. Before setting out, double-check the route using the latest map, weather forecast, and local announcements.

## Ölüdeniz–Hisarçandır 430 km route record

> Historical map note: The route descriptions and record in this section relate to preparations I made in September 2014, and should not be used as current passage or service information.

The Lycian Way runs from Fethiye to Antalya. According to my 2014 notes, you could start in Ölüdeniz or walk the other way from Hisarçandır. The route occasionally followed roads but mostly kept to trails with red-and-white markers. I studied each stage separately because difficulty, water, accommodation and food varied along the way.

Thank you to the likyayolu.org team for creating and sharing this 430 km single-line GPS recording. The fact that the total distance of 509 km includes alternative routes that split and rejoin at certain points is why the distance in the single-line recording appears shorter.

I spent a long time working on the route before the hike. I worked out where the paths ran, where I’d sleep, how far I’d walk each day and which parts would tire me out most. I could already picture the whole route on the map. I’d get up at sunrise and walk until 18:00 at the latest. It got dark at 19:00, and finding a place to pitch a tent could take time. I had 21 days and planned to walk on 16 of them. I’d walk for 4 days and rest for 1, finishing in 19 days with 2 days to spare. On paper it looked great. I’d get food and supplies along the way. It didn’t go like that in practice.

I gathered these details while researching the trip and carried handwritten notes in case I needed them on the trail. What you see here is my own plan. The most help came from Özlem Akovalıgil, who had walked many parts of the route, and from the Lycian Way guidebook.

## Day by day, my Lycian Way plan.

**Day 1 (20 September 2014)**

Ölüdeniz → Faralya → Butterfly Valley → Kabak Bay (25 km)

- It looks like 15 km from Ölüdeniz to Faralya, 3.5 km from Faralya to Butterfly Valley, and 6.5 km from Butterfly Valley to Kabak Bay.
- 2.5 km ahead, there will be approximately 500 meters of elevation within 3 km. This could be a bit tiring.
- After that, the route starts descending. There is no water until Kozağaç village.
- Hikers have lost their way during the descent from Kirme to Faralya. It’s important to proceed carefully to avoid losing the path.
- At the entrance to Faralya, there’s a lovely, helpful aunt who works at the shop. I should definitely stop by.
- Between Faralya and Kabak Bay, the trail turns left into the forest. The turn is easy to miss, so watch for it.
- From Faralya I planned to go down into Butterfly Valley and climb back out. The descent is steep and people use ropes. Going down and back up with a backpack would be hard.
- I’m planning to spend the first night in my tent in the woods near the beach at Kabak Bay.

**Day 2 (21 September 2014)**

Kabak Bay -> Alınca -> Sidyma Antik Kent -> Yediburunlar (31.1 km)

- Kabak Bay to Alınca is 15.6 km, Alınca to Sidyma is 7.5 km, and Sidyma to Yediburunlar is approximately 8 km.
- There’s a camping area on Kabak cove’s beach. Apparently, the price went down from 30 to 25TL, and it’s necessary to bargain.
- After Kabak, there’s a steep slope leading up to Alınca.
- I’m planning to spend the night in a suitable place in Yediburunlar, in a tent.

**Day 3 (22 September 2014)**

Yediburunlar -> Gavurağalı -> Patara (27 km)

- The distance between Yediburunlar and Gavurağalı is approximately 13 km, while the distance between Gavurağalı and Patara is approximately 14 km.
- I’m planning to spend the night setting up a tent in a suitable spot in Patara, or at the Medusa campsite.
- At Medusa campsite, there are toilets/showers. The tent fee may be 10 TL.
- When you pass Medusa, there are open spaces to the right ahead. These could also be used to set up a tent.
- You can reach the beach by walking from the ancient city of Patara.
- You should watch the sunset at Patara beach.
- In Patara, there’s a local eatery run by small businesses, offering home-cooked meals alongside the tourism business of Dardanos. The food is good. Should I try it? Should you try it? :)
- In Patara, you can find everything you’re looking for – from a local grocery store and market to a bar.

4th Day (September 23, 2014)

Kınık -> Çavdar -> Çayköy -> İslamlar -> Gelemiş (33 km)

- The distance between Kınık and Çavdar is 5 km, while the distance between Çavdar and Çayköy-İslamlar is 12 km. The route from İslamlar to Gelemiş is approximately 16 km.
- I’m planning to spend the night in a tent or a guesthouse in Gelemiş.
- After walking for 4 days, I’ve earned a day to lie in. I’ll spend a day around Gelemiş.

With that, I’m completing the first part of the race, which is 120 km long.

**Day 5 (25 September 2014)**

Gelemiş -> Kalkan -> Bezirgan -> Kaputaş Canyon -> Sarıbelen (38 km)

- The distance between Gelemiş and Kalkan is 20 km, the distance between Kalkan and Bezirgan is 8 km, the distance between Bezirgan and Kaputaş Kanyonu is 6 km, and the distance between Kaputaş Kanyonu and Sarıbelen is approximately 4 km.
- The elevation will continue until reaching the Bezirgan. Afterwards, a flat road appears.
- 38 km can be completed in one day, but I think I can succeed because I’ll take a day off.
- I’ll spend the night camping in a suitable place near Sarıbelen.

**Day 6 (26 September 2014)**

Sarıbelen -> Gökçeören -> Çukurbağ (33 km)

- The distance between Sarıbelen and Gökçeören is 12 km, while the distance between Gökçeören and Çukurbağ is 21 km.
- I plan to spend the night setting up a tent in Çukurbağ in a suitable location.

**Day 7 (27 September 2014)**

Çukurbağ → Kaş → Körmen Island → Üçağız (Aperlai) (32 km)

- The distance between Çukurbağ and Kaş is 7 km, the distance between Kaş and Körmen Island is 13 km, and the distance between Körmen Island and Üçağız (Aperlai) is approximately 12 km.
- The route descends from Çukurbağ to Kaş and then follows the coast.
- I plan to spend the night by setting up a tent in Üçağız in a suitable place.
- I plan to walk over 30 km in the last 3 days. With the 8-9 months of exercise I’ve been doing, it feels like I’ll be able to achieve that.

8th Day (September 28, 2014)

Üçağız (Aperlai) -> Kale Üçağız -> Kapaklı -> Demre (Bird Paradise) (28 km)

- The distance between Üçağız (Aperlai) and Kale is 8 km, the distance between Kale and Kapaklı is 9 km, and the distance between Kapaklı and Demre (Bird Paradise) is approximately 11 km.
- It stretches from the coast towards Demre. There aren’t many ups and downs.
- I plan to spend the night in Demre, either by setting up a tent in a suitable place or by staying in a guesthouse.
- I’m sure that these 4 days will be very tiring. I’ll be very happy to have one day of rest.

  On stage 2 I’ll cover 131 km of the route, bringing my total to 251 km.

9th Day (September 30, 2014)

Demre -> Alakilise -> Belos -> Finike (37 km)

- The distance between Demre and Alakilise is 12 km, the distance between Alakilise and Belos is 15 km, and the distance between Belos and Finike is approximately 10 km.
- The route climbs from Demre to Belos, then descends.
- I plan to spend the night setting up a tent in Finike, in a suitable location.

10th Day (October 1, 2014)

Finike -> Kumluca -> Karaöz (30 km)

- The distance between Finike and Kumluca is 15 km, and the distance between Kumluca and Karaöz is also 15 km.
- This section continues from the coast.
- I plan to spend the night setting up a tent in Karaöz, in a suitable location.

11th Day (October 2, 2014)

Karaöz → Gelidonya Lighthouse → Adrasan → Olympos (Çıralı) (33 km)

- The distances in my plan were 7 km from Karaöz to Gelidonya Lighthouse, 12 km from Gelidonya Lighthouse to Adrasan and 14 km from Adrasan to Olympos (Çıralı).
- Continuing from the coast to Adrasan.
- There is no water on the Gelidonya Lighthouse trail.
- I’m planning to spend the night in my tent somewhere suitable in Olimpus.

12th Day (October 3, 2014)

Çıralı -> Tekirova (20 km)

- The distance between Çıralı and Tekirova appears to be 20 km.
- Continuing from the coast.
- I plan to spend the night in Tekirova, either by setting up a tent in a suitable location or by staying at a guesthouse.

  On stage 3 I’ll cover 120 km of the route, bringing my total to 371 km.

13th Day (October 5, 2014)

Tekirova -> Çıralı (20 km)

- I’m returning along the same path I took. There’s a route here within the course, and I’ll be returning along the same path. I don’t want to deviate from the course, so I’m returning.
- Continuing from the coast.
- I plan to spend the night in Çıralı, setting up a tent in a suitable location.

14th Day (October 6, 2014)

Olimpus (Çıralı) -> Beycik -> Gedelme (34 km)

- The distance between Olimpus (Çıralı) and Beycik is 16 km, while the distance between Beycik and Gedelme is approximately 18 km.
- After Beycik, the route climbs to 1,800 metres, gaining 1,000 metres over 5 km. I think this will be the hardest section. After that, it’s downhill.
- I plan to spend the night by setting up a tent in Gedelme, in a suitable place.

15th Day (October 7, 2014)

Gedelme -> Göynük (20 km)

- The route continues downhill.
- I plan to spend the night in Göynük, setting up a tent in a suitable location.

16th Day (October 8, 2014)

Göynük to Hisarçandır (20 km)

- Initially, there’s an upward trend, followed by a downward decline. This section will be quite demanding.
- I plan to spend the night by setting up a tent in Hisarçandır, in a suitable place, or by staying at a guesthouse.

  On stage 4 I’ll cover 94 km of the route, bringing my total to 465 km.

 

Additional Notes;

- The full Lycian Way is 509 km. My planned walk was 465 km. I checked the difference because I thought I might have made a mistake. The full figure counts alternative branches where the route splits and later rejoins.
- I’ve allowed 16 days of walking and 3 days of rest, using 19 of my 21 days. I expect to need the other 2 days somewhere along the trail. If I don’t use them, I’ll put my feet up somewhere after I finish.
- In the park, there may be a shortage of water and food. I need to be careful not to get dehydrated.
- All of the plans look great on paper. Let's see how they work in reality.
