Going trekking alone requires courage. There’s no need to explain the reason in detail – you already know it. Going alone is always significantly riskier. Especially if you are a woman, it’s incredibly difficult, even just to imagine doing it. It’s a very sad situation to feel unsafe. As a man, it’s easier to go alone, but there have been times when, even then, I’ve experienced the same anxiety. I don’t have a fear of nature, but when I encounter a person, I always have a sense of uncertainty. The question of what I would do if I encountered a dangerous person – rather than “what would I do if I encountered a bear?” – has really made me think. I don’t generalize and try to avoid every person I see, of course, but when I witness events, I need to be cautious. When I’m at the top of the mountain, going alone with my backpack, I become anxious when I see someone – someone whose presence isn’t part of my plan – and that’s not because I’m afraid of every person I see.

Let’s turn to the book titled **Solo Nature Hike** written by Adrienne Hall. I searched for the author’s biography online, but I couldn’t reach her. The publishing house that translated the book mentioned that she lives in Alaka and is a professional nature walker, having studied biology and also completed a master’s degree in environmental science. So, she’s someone like us who enjoys nature walks. While she doesn’t just settle for going on solo hikes, she’s created a truly excellent book. Going on a solo hike is very different from going with a group – both the risks and the enjoyment are quite different. The book first discusses the fact that you should go alone, and then details what kind of preparation you should make, and mentions the important first aid supplies you should have with you. It also explains how to deal with the difficulties you might encounter when hiking alone. The rest of the book then focuses on safety-related topics. It describes what you should do if you are injured, or if a snake bites you, and even explains how wrong it is to try to spit out the venom if a snake bites you – a piece of information that the reader will find useful. What should you do? Let me give you the answer. If a snake bites you, you should immediately seek emergency assistance. Don’t try to spit out the venom – your saliva will actually spread it more quickly. Finally, the book covers everything that could happen on a solo hiking trip. In short, it effectively explains the differences between going on a solo hike and going with a group, and is a valuable guide for anyone who wants to go on a solo hike. I definitely recommend it as a must-have in any nature walker’s library.
