## Sidyma–Karadere: Gavurağalı’s Steep Descent

This page focuses on the third stage, covering a 17 km route from Sidyma to Gavurağalı and on to Karadere. The difficult and risky descent in Gavurağalı, described as the most challenging and dangerous section of the hike during the first three days of the 2014 excursion.

> Historical field record: The following practical information is based on the experience of 22 September 2014. Water, facility, access, and camping conditions should not be considered current information.

### Stage summary

- **Stage:** 3 of 11
- **Start and finish:** Sidyma – Karadere
- **Waypoints:** Gavurağalı and the coastal plain near Patara
- **Distance:** 17 km based on signposted distances
- **Walking time:** The total walking time was not recorded.
- **Walked:** 22 September 2014
- **Current conditions:** No recent verification was available for this guide.

### Terrain, difficulty and navigation

The text describes a 2014 recording, detailing the loss of markers following the Sidyma event and two hours spent on a forest slope outside the route. Access to Gavurağalı is via a steep, rocky descent; the author recounts falling twice during the descent, followed by a rest break. Afterwards, the route continued along the coastal flat.

### Water and food — 2014 record

Following the landing in Sidyma, there was no record of water being logged between the landing site and the cemetery. The author noted a record of carrying 3 liters of water based on this historical experience. Karadere’deki restoran was described as the initial food and logistical point within the area. The current status of the source is unknown.

### Accommodation, camping and ways off the route — 2014 record

It has been noted that a tent was set up near the Özlen restaurant/guesthouse in Karadere. There are no other recorded settlements or exits between Sidyma and Karadere; the current status of the mentioned establishment was not verified.

### Notable places

Sidyma Ancient City, the Gavurağalı landing, the coastal flatland, and the coastline facing Patara are prominent features in this area.

### What I learned on this stage

The third day’s log reveals that attempting to shortcut the route by going beyond the marked path, when a sign is lost, leads to exhaustion and increased risk. The author considers deviating from the route to be the most important mistake of the day.

- There’s a steep, rocky descent to Gavurağalı. Take care. I fell 2 times and barely stopped myself from sliding farther down.
- After the steep slope levels out, the route continues along the coast.
- As you continue towards Patara, following the slope, there’s a cemetery further ahead. Water can be obtained from here.
- There is no water from Sidyma to this point. It would be beneficial to have 3 liters of water with you.
- There’s a restaurant/guesthouse called Özlen in Karadere. I spent the night there. There’s no camping fee. I actually set up my tent on a rocky ground. Frankly, this place didn’t feel very safe.
- Up to this restaurant, there’s nothing on the road. You can obtain the logistical support here for the first time.
- There are no settlements beyond Karadere.
- In Sidyma, there’s no cafe or market.

I had calculated the route length based on the values on the signs. However, since the drawing on Wikiloc is based on a map, there are differences.

Note: This information relates to a hike on September 22, 2014.
