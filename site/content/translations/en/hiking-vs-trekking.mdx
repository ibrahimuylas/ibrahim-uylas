Trekking and hiking both mean walking in nature. The difference usually comes down to how long you walk, whether you need overnight stops and how much you carry. Hiking is usually a day trip with a lighter pack, while trekking is a journey you divide into stages over several days. Different countries and organisations use the terms differently.

## Duration and overnight stops

A hike can be long or difficult and still finish the same day. On a trek, the journey continues the next day. That makes where you sleep, how you refill water and restock food, and when you finish each stage part of your plan.

## Pack and equipment

For most day hikes, a small pack with water, food, a rain layer, navigation and emergency supplies is enough. On a trek, you may also need overnight equipment, more food, spare clothing and sometimes a way to treat water. Let the route determine pack size and weight. A larger pack does not automatically make the walk easier.

Use the [hiking gear checklist](/doga-yuruyusleri-icin-gerekli-ekipmanlar/) to prepare.

## Route and pace

You can hike or trek on marked trails and on rough terrain. Fatigue builds across several days on a trek, so plan daily distance and elevation more conservatively. Instead of aiming for a particular speed, choose a stage you can finish safely in daylight.

## Which one should I choose?

- If this is your first walk in nature, start with a short, marked hiking route.
- Plan a hike if you want to carry a light pack and come back the same day.
- Plan a trek if you want to take several days to follow a route and stop overnight.
- Try your equipment on short routes before attempting a multi-day walk.
- Whatever you call the walk, check the forecast, route, water and plans for getting back before you go.

## Before you leave

Check route information against current sources, share your plan with someone you trust, and allow enough time to turn back if you encounter unexpected weather or ground conditions.
