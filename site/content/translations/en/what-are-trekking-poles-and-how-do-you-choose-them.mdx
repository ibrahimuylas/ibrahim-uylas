I have examined the statistical data regarding the effect of using a walking stick during hikes on the feet. Experts have even performed the calculations themselves. They noted that when carrying a load of 34 tonnes in an hour, using a walking stick means transferring a 13 tonne load from the feet and placing it manually on the ground. Such tonnage figures may not readily form an image in your mind, nor did they for me. I can report the following from my own experience: when I use the walking stick only on the right side during hikes, my left foot becomes significantly more tired; conversely, when I use the walking stick only on the left side during a hike, my right foot becomes significantly more tired. Furthermore, when I do not use a walking stick, I find that I become tired much more quickly and intensely.

My recommendation for long walks is to use two poles. If you’re experienced, you’ll also use two poles. Another advantage is using poles to support yourself when walking on wet/slippery surfaces, or when crossing areas with varying elevation. If you don’t have poles, there are plenty of trees along the path you’re walking on. You can make your own poles from them – use dried, broken branches. Don’t go and break living trees.

The biggest advantage of trekking poles is that they are made of aluminum or carbon. Their strength and lightness make them feel weightless while you’re walking. Don’t say “I don’t need a pole” when you find a suitable branch on the trail – it will quickly become a burden to carry. These guys have thought about it and done it right.

## How should a hiking pole be chosen?

There are two types of batons. One is fixed, and the other is telescopic – meaning they can be extended. The fixed ones take up space, making it difficult to store them and requiring you to find the right length based on your height. With the telescopic ones, you can adjust the length to suit the situation, such as when descending or ascending slopes, allowing for more effective use. The telescopic ones come in two varieties: 2-stage and 3-stage. The 3-stage models are more robust and have a longer lifespan compared to the 2-stage models.

Another important point to consider is that the batons’ handles are made of ergonomic and mushroom-shaped material. This material makes them softer and prevents your hand from getting wet, while the ergonomic design allows you to hold them comfortably and transfer their weight more effectively.

My recommendation is to choose a carbon fiber baton – a three-stage, ergonomic model with a mushroom-shaped handle.
