I’d read a few things about how much of a difference trekking poles make to your feet. The experts had even done the maths. Something about your feet carrying a total load of 34 tonnes in an hour, and poles taking 13 tonnes of that and transferring it to the ground through your hands. If you can’t picture that in tonnes, neither can I. Here’s what I can tell you from experience: walk with a pole only on my right and my left foot tires first. Swap sides and it’s my right foot that feels it. Leave the poles at home and I tire more quickly, full stop.

For a long walk I’d take two poles. Watch the old hands and you’ll see they use two as well. Poles give you something to lean on when the ground’s wet or slippery, when you’re breaking trail in snow, or when you’re stepping between different levels. Haven’t got a pole? Look for a stick along the trail. I mean a dead branch that’s already fallen. Don’t go breaking one off a living tree.

The biggest advantage of trekking poles is that they are made of aluminium or carbon. Their strength and lightness mean they don’t become a burden while you’re walking. A branch you find on the trail might seem fine at first, but after a while, its weight will become a burden. Carrying it becomes a job in itself. So don’t say, “I can manage with a stick, what do I need poles for?” The big brothers thought about it and made them.

## How should a hiking pole be chosen?

Poles come in two types, fixed-length and telescopic. Fixed-length poles are awkward to pack and you have to buy the right length for your height. With telescopic poles you can set the length yourself and adjust it again for a climb or descent. They come in two or three sections. I find the three-section ones sturdier and longer-lasting than the two-section models.

Pay attention to the grips too. I look for cork grips that fit the hand comfortably. Cork feels softer and helps prevent blisters. With a grip that fits well, it’s easier to put your weight through the pole.

My pick would be a three-section carbon pole with a comfortable, shaped cork grip.
