12 March 2013

Approximately 10 days ago, I returned from the Kilimli bay camping trip. The weather was rainy, and I was completely covered in filthy mud. When I got home, I was going to wash my boots, but then I noticed that about 10 days had passed, and my boots were still like a mass of mud. While I was brushing my boots in warm water, watching the mud flow, I suddenly felt a sense of sadness. I realized that I hadn't given them any attention on my travels for a long time. They had been waiting for me for 10 days, sitting on the shelf, covered in mud. I confessed that when I was planning a new camping trip, I remembered that my boots were muddy and needed washing. It felt bad to realize that I hadn't been giving them the attention they deserved, especially since they had been so important to me, protecting my feet from the ground and hard objects during my travels, keeping them dry, even in the rain and mud. My boots and other gear, I promise to take much better care of you, because I have to – it’s essential for our continued relationship :)

This applies to other aspects of our lives as well, doesn’t it? We tend to dwell on our shortcomings and things beyond our control. We only realize the value of what we have when we lose it. Through this brief conversation with my bots, I’ve once again realized the value of the people and things that are important to me. I hope we always remember the value of what we have. Wishing you good health.

19 March 2020

During the times when COVID-19 seemed to be spreading throughout the streets of London, and I was carefully taking walks every weekend, my boots became completely covered in mud each time, and I had to wash them every week. London, after all, is known for its rain and mud.
